Bandit: Plane Spotter

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 30 September 2026

Bandit is a game about photographing aircraft. This policy explains what the app does with your account, progress, camera, location and photographs, and what your squad and friends can see.

The short version
  • You can play without an account. Signed-out progress and photographs remain on your device.
  • If you create an account, Bandit uses Google Firebase to sign you in, publish your display name and scores, run squads and friends, and back up your cards, scores, logbook and other game progress, including compressed copies of your photographs.
  • Location is used to find nearby aircraft and is stored with logged sightings. Signed-in players include those sighting records in their private backup.
  • Global rankings show your chosen display name and game statistics to other signed-in players. Squad members and friends can see the information described below. They see your photographs only if you turn on Binder sharing.
  • Bandit has no advertising and does not track you across apps or websites.

Signing in and game progress

Bandit starts without an account and remains playable signed out. Cloud backup, global rankings, squads and friends require an account. Creating an account uses the email address, password and public display name you provide. Google Firebase Authentication handles the password; Bandit never receives it in readable form.

When you are signed in, Bandit stores your email address and authentication record in Firebase Authentication. Cloud Firestore stores your display name, cards, scores, achievements, purchases made with earned in-game currency, logbook entries, spotting sessions, squad membership, friends and related game progress. This data is linked to your account so it can be restored on another device.

Global rankings are visible only to signed-in players. They contain your chosen display name and game statistics: lifetime spot, cash and XP totals, level, prestige, badge count, counts of the aircraft types, tail numbers and rarities you have collected, recent daily totals and the time the score was updated. They do not contain your email address, photographs, notes or location.

Camera and photographs

Bandit takes a photograph when you press the shutter and saves it in the app's private storage on your device. Google's ML Kit image-labeling library checks for an aircraft on the device using a model bundled in the app. Photographs are not sent to ML Kit or to another image-analysis service.

When you are signed in, Bandit also backs up a copy of each photograph to your account in Firebase Storage so it can be restored on another device. Backup copies are JPEG images no more than 2048 pixels on the longer side, and camera metadata, including any embedded location, is removed before upload. The originals on your phone are not changed. Only your account can read its backup copies. Signed out, photographs stay on the device where they were taken.

Location

While the Spot screen is open, Bandit uses your location to:

  1. Find nearby aircraft. Bandit asks one of two aircraft-tracking services and falls back to the other when it is unavailable: ADSB.lol, which receives coordinates rounded to two decimal places, and the OpenSky Network. Signed-in players with a verified app reach OpenSky through Bandit's Firebase traffic service in Australia, which keeps the OpenSky credential out of the app. Signed-out or unverified apps ask OpenSky directly. Direct traffic-feed requests contain no Bandit account or device identifier.
  2. Name the place where a photograph was taken, using the geocoding service provided by the operating system. Google provides this service on Android.
  3. Save the location of a logged sighting. Signed-in players include this record in their private Firebase progress backup.
  4. Check for interesting aircraft near the last location used while the app was open, if alerts are enabled. On Android, Bandit does not request background-location permission and discards a remembered alert location after six hours.

On iPhone, if you turn on aircraft alerts and choose to allow location access “Always”, Bandit also uses your location while the app is closed: iOS tells Bandit when your phone has moved a significant distance, and Bandit uses that position, or your current position when it checks in the background, to look for interesting aircraft near you. That position is used only for the nearby-traffic request described in item 1 and to decide whether to show an alert on your phone. It is not added to your sightings, your backup or your squad, and is not otherwise uploaded. You can turn this off at any time in the iPhone Settings app under Bandit, or by turning off aircraft alerts.

The radar and logbook maps show satellite imagery from Esri's ArcGIS World Imagery service. Each map-tile request tells Esri which area is being shown — around you on the radar, or around a logged sighting in the logbook — and, like any web request, your IP address. It contains no Bandit account or device identifier.

Squads, friends and Binder sharing

A squad is a small group joined with an invitation code. Squad members can see each other's chosen display names and scores. Squad sightings include the aircraft, callsign when available, time, place name and coordinates of the sighting.

You can also add other signed-in players as friends. A friend request or squad invitation shows your display name to the player you send it to. Friends can see your profile: your display name, prestige, badges, featured aircraft and showcase card, and the cosmetics you have equipped.

Binder sharing is optional and starts off. If you turn it on, your squad members and friends can see the cards you share: allowlisted details such as aircraft identity, rarity, condition, capture date and flight details, and a reduced-size copy of each card's photograph, stored in Firebase Storage with camera metadata, including location, removed. Notes, account email, balance, full-size photographs and your private backup are not shared. Access ends when you turn sharing off, leave the squad or remove the friend.

Aircraft identification and alerts

Bandit sends an aircraft's public Mode-S hex code, registration or callsign to adsbdb.com, ADSB.lol, the OpenSky Network and planespotters.net to find its type, registration, route, the path it has flown and a photograph of it. These requests contain no information about you.

Alerts are composed on the device from aircraft data already fetched by Bandit. There is no advertising or marketing notification service.

Storage, security and retention

Firebase encrypts data in transit and at rest. Firestore and Storage rules restrict private backup records and photo copies to the signed-in account, restrict squad data to current squad members, and restrict shared cards to current squad members and friends while Binder sharing is on. Global ranking fields are deliberately limited to the fields described above.

Bandit keeps cloud account data while the account exists. Backup revisions may retain earlier versions of game progress and photo copies so restores are reliable. Deleting the account deletes the Firebase Authentication account and associated Firebase data, including photo copies. We do not retain account data after deletion unless required by law or needed briefly to complete the deletion request.

Deleting data or an account

Remove local data by uninstalling Bandit or, on Android, using Settings → Apps → Bandit → Storage → Clear storage.

To delete a Bandit account and its Firebase data, follow the account deletion instructions. You may email banditplanespotter@gmail.com from the account address with the subject “Delete my Bandit account”. Requests are completed within 30 days. Signing out does not delete an account.

Advertising, tracking and children

Bandit shows no advertising, does not sell personal information and does not track people across apps or websites. Bandit is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect their information.
